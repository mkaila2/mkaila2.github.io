Product Discovery · Data Visualization · Public Safety

Helping cities understand pedestrian rail risk.

Exploring a data-driven decision tool to help identify potentially dangerous pedestrian railroad crossings.

PartnerOlsson / Raikes Design Studio
RoleProject contributor · Discovery and Agile coordination
FocusCustomer discovery · Dashboard strategy
Discovery insight
The right solution starts with understanding who will actually use it.

Context

Our Design Studio team is developing a dashboard to help surface potentially dangerous pedestrian railroad crossings, with particular attention to routes students use when walking to school.

The initial assumption

Early in the project, railroad companies were considered a likely primary customer group. Customer discovery encouraged us to revisit that framing and consider city planners as a more relevant audience for the dashboard.

My contributions

  • Helped lead customer discovery and translate feedback into product direction.
  • Led weekly team stand-ups and supported coordination through Trello.
  • Worked with teammates on refining who the dashboard is for and what decisions it should support.

What changed

Reframing the customer from railroads toward city planners changes the questions a dashboard needs to answer, such as how municipal stakeholders might prioritize locations for further safety assessment.

What I learned

Being willing to question your first customer hypothesis is a core product skill. Discovery is valuable when it changes what you build—not just when it confirms what you already believe.

This is an in-progress student project. Avoid describing the dashboard as launched or claiming safety outcomes without verified evidence and permission to share.
