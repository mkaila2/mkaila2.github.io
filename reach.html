Turning customer discovery into ACT prep app requirements.

How conversations with school administrators informed the product direction of Reach, a student-led test-preparation startup.

OrganizationReach / Raikes Startup Studio
RoleProduct Management Intern
WhenSep – Dec 2025
Product principle
Build from what users need, not what we assume they need.

Context

Reach was developing an ACT preparation app. Ahead of its planned January launch, the founding team wanted clearer insights into how schools might use the product and what administrators would value.

The challenge

Product decisions made before launch can be shaped by assumptions. We needed to better understand school administrators’ workflows, priorities, and pain points so their perspectives could inform feature requirements.

My role

As a Product Management Intern working with the founding team, I supported customer discovery and the translation of feedback into actionable product insights.

My approach

  • Reached out to school administrators to gather perspectives on their needs and workflows.
  • Conducted structured interviews to uncover pain points and recurring themes.
  • Synthesized interview feedback into product insights for the founding team.
  • Translated customer needs into proposed feature requirements ahead of the app’s January launch.

Product impact

The discovery work helped inform the app’s feature requirements before launch. This case study does not claim quantified adoption or business results that have not been verified.

What I learned

Customer discovery becomes valuable when research turns into decisions. Asking thoughtful questions and synthesizing patterns helps a team choose what to build next.

Before publishing, verify the launch timing and ensure these details are appropriate to share publicly.
